question: Can you take paracetamol and ibuprofen together?
---
Yes—for most adults, paracetamol (acetaminophen) and ibuprofen can be taken together if needed, as they work differently and do not directly interact. The NHS advises trying one medicine first, using the lowest effective dose, and stopping when pain or fever settles.

How to take them safely
Paracetamol: Typically 500–1,000 mg every 4–6 hours as needed; do not exceed 4 g in 24 hours unless a clinician advises otherwise.

Ibuprofen: Typically 200–400 mg every 4–6 hours as needed; for over-the-counter use, do not exceed 1,200 mg in 24 hours unless your clinician/pharmacist advises otherwise.

You may take them at the same time or stagger doses (for example, paracetamol first, then ibuprofen 2–3 hours later). Keep track of timings to avoid accidental overdose.

Take ibuprofen with food or milk to reduce stomach irritation.

Important cautions
Do not combine ibuprofen with other NSAIDs such as aspirin or naproxen, as this raises the risk of stomach ulcers and bleeding.

Avoid ibuprofen or ask a clinician first if you have a history of stomach ulcer/bleeding, kidney disease, significant heart disease, uncontrolled high blood pressure, or certain asthma triggers.

Do not take extra paracetamol from other products—many cold/flu remedies contain it—and excess paracetamol can cause serious liver injury.

Seek medical advice before combining them if you are pregnant, especially after 28 weeks; ibuprofen is generally not recommended in the third trimester.

When to get help
Contact a pharmacist, doctor, or urgent care service if pain/fever persists beyond a few days, worsens, or you need both medicines regularly. Get urgent help for signs such as black/tarry stools, vomiting blood, severe stomach pain, allergic reaction, or suspected overdose.
