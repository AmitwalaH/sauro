question: How much caffeine per day is considered safe for adults?
---
For most healthy adults, the widely cited safe limit is about 400 mg of caffeine per day. That's roughly four 8-ounce cups of brewed coffee, and it's the benchmark most major health organizations agree on. The figure comes from the U.S. FDA and from the European Food Safety Authority, which found that single doses up to 200 mg and daily intakes up to 400 mg don't raise safety concerns for adults.

A few groups should stay lower:

Pregnant women: up to 200 mg a day doesn't raise safety concerns for the fetus, so that's the usual ceiling.
newhope
Children and adolescents: EFSA puts it at 3 mg per kg of body weight per day, which for most teens works out to roughly 100 mg or less.
newhope
People with heart conditions, anxiety disorders, sleep problems, or on certain medications: often advised to limit further; some people feel wired and queasy at 150 mg, so individual tolerance matters a lot.
acibademinternational
For context on what counts toward 400 mg: brewed coffee is roughly 95–100 mg per 8 oz, a large coffeehouse coffee 250–330 mg, an espresso shot about 65 mg, black tea about 45–50 mg, and energy drinks anywhere from 70–300 mg. A single large café coffee can get you most of the way to the limit. Timing matters too, since caffeine has a roughly five-hour half-life, so a 200 mg coffee at 3 p.m. still leaves about 50 mg in your system at 1 a.m.
acibademinternational
acibademinternational

Note that 400 mg is an upper bound considered safe, not a target—and if you have a medical condition, your doctor may advise something different.
