question: Is it safe to take ibuprofen with blood pressure medication?
---
Ibuprofen can interact with blood pressure medication, so taking them together is not always safe. It depends on which BP medication you're taking, your dosage, and how frequently you use ibuprofen.

Ibuprofen is an NSAID (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug), commonly sold under brands such as Brufen and Advil.

What are the risks?
Taking ibuprofen with certain blood pressure medicines can:

Increase blood pressure: Ibuprofen can reduce the effectiveness of some BP medications.

Affect kidney function: The combination can put extra stress on your kidneys.

Cause fluid retention: This can worsen high blood pressure or heart failure.

The risk is particularly concerning when ibuprofen is combined with:

ACE inhibitors (e.g. ramipril, enalapril).

ARBs (e.g. telmisartan, losartan, olmesartan).

Diuretics (e.g. hydrochlorothiazide, furosemide).

Is there a safer alternative?
For ordinary pain or fever, paracetamol (Dolo 650, Crocin, or Calpol) may be a more suitable alternative for many people with high blood pressure. However, it also needs to be used according to the recommended dose and individual medical circumstances.

Avoid taking ibuprofen regularly without checking with your doctor or pharmacist first.
