question: What happened in the most recent SpaceX Starship test?
---
SpaceX completed Starship Flight 15 (IFT-15) on November 13–14, 2026, achieving a historic milestone: for the first time ever, the launch tower's mechanical "chopsticks" arms (Mechazilla) successfully caught the Starship upper stage (Ship 42) returning directly from orbit.

However, SpaceX missed out on its goal of an unprecedented "double catch" because the Super Heavy booster (Booster 22) malfunctioned during its descent shortly after liftoff and had to be diverted.

Key Mission Breakdown
Liftoff & Stage Separation (Nov 13): The rocket lifted off smoothly from Starbase, Texas at 5:47 p.m. EST (4:17 a.m. IST on Nov 14). All 33 Raptor engines on the booster fired at liftoff, and hot-staging separation proceeded cleanly.

Booster Abort & Explosion: As Booster 22 began its return toward the launch pad, it suffered an engine dropout and a guidance/landing anomaly. Flight computers automatically aborted the tower catch and diverted the booster offshore into the Gulf of Mexico, where it triggered its flight termination system and exploded.

Orbital Insertion & Satellite Deployment: Ship 42 fired all 6 of its engines to reach a near-circular low Earth orbit at roughly 275 km altitude—only the second time Starship has reached orbit. Once in orbit, it successfully deployed 26 operational Starlink V3 satellites (three of which carried cameras to inspect Ship's heat shield tiles from space).

Historic Upper-Stage Catch (Nov 14): After circling Earth 14 times over roughly 22.5 hours, flight controllers verified that Ship's heat shield and flight surfaces were healthy enough for a pad return. Ship 42 survived the intense heat of atmospheric reentry, executed its landing flip, and was plucked out of midair by Mechazilla's arms at Pad 2.

Why It Matters
While SpaceX had previously caught Super Heavy boosters on earlier flights, catching the orbital upper stage is far more challenging because it returns from space at hypersonic speeds through extreme reentry heating. Pulling this off proves that the upper stage can be recovered intact, bringing SpaceX one step closer to a fully and rapidly reusable orbital launch system needed for its Starlink V3 rollout and NASA's Artemis lunar missions.
