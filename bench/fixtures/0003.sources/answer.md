question: What are the early symptoms of type 2 diabetes?
---
Early symptoms of type 2 diabetes often develop very gradually—so slowly that many people have the condition for years without realizing it.

Common Early Symptoms
Increased thirst and frequent urination: High blood sugar forces your kidneys to work extra hard to filter out excess glucose. This pulls fluids from your body tissue, leaving you dehydrated and making you drink and urinate much more than usual (especially at night).

Constant fatigue: Because your body's cells cannot efficiently take in glucose from your blood to use for fuel, you can feel chronically tired and weak, even if you are sleeping well.

Increased hunger (polyphagia): Even after you eat, your cells remain starved of energy because glucose can't enter them without working insulin, leading to ongoing hunger.

Unintentional weight loss: When your body cannot use sugar for energy, it begins burning muscle and fat for fuel instead, causing weight loss even if you are eating normally or more.

Blurry vision: High blood sugar draws fluid out of the lenses of your eyes, affecting your ability to focus clearly.

Slow-healing cuts or frequent infections: Elevated blood sugar impairs circulation and immune function, making cuts, sores, and skin or yeast infections heal much slower than normal.

Tingling or numbness: High glucose over time damages small nerves, leading to a "pins and needles" feeling or numbness in your hands and feet.

Dark patches of skin (Acanthosis Nigricans): Darker, velvety patches of skin can form on body folds, most commonly around the neck, armpits, or groin area. This is a common sign of insulin resistance.
